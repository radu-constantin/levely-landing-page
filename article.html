Why I’m building a focus timer that levels you up

Every focus app I’ve used hands you a cartoon plant. I wanted the reward to be the thing I was actually trying to get better at, so I started building it.

R
Radu
Building Levely in public

I have started roughly forty things this year. I have finished four of them. The gap between those two numbers is the entire reason this app exists, and if you’re reading this you probably have your own version of those numbers.

For a while I blamed my timer. That was unfair to the timer. Pomodoro apps do exactly what they say: they count down from twenty-five and then they make a noise. The problem is what happens after the noise. You get a checkmark, or a streak, or a tiny tree in a little forest, and none of it has anything to do with the thing you just spent twenty-five minutes on.

The plant can’t play guitar

This is the sentence I kept coming back to, and it’s stayed as the pitch because I haven’t found a shorter way to say it. The plant is a proxy. It grows when you focus, so watching it grow feels a bit like progress, and your brain accepts the trade for a while. Then one day you notice you’ve grown a very healthy digital shrub and you still can’t play the song.

What I wanted was a timer where the reward and the work were the same object. You sit down for forty minutes of guitar, and what levels up is guitar. Not a shrub. Not a streak counter. The skill itself, named the way you named it, moving because you moved it.

Skill by use

The mechanic is old and it isn’t mine. Some role-playing games level a skill by making you use that skill — you don’t spend points on swimming, you get better at swimming by swimming. I’ve always liked how honest that is. There’s no build to plan and no way to game it. You get good at what you actually do.

Applied to a focus timer it turns out to be almost embarrassingly simple. You define the skills you care about. You start a session and tell it which skill you’re working. When the session ends, that skill takes the XP. Nothing else in the app earns anything. If you want a level, you have to go and do the thing.

No XP for opening the app. No bonus for a perfect week. No penalty for a broken streak — a broken streak is already punishment enough, and I refuse to build software that scolds people.

I’ve cut a lot of ideas that fought with this. The rules I keep coming back to:

  • XP comes from finished sessions, nothing else.
  • Every session belongs to exactly one skill.
  • The app never invents a skill for you.

Why I’m writing this down

This is my first iOS app, I’m building it alone, and I’ve given myself a hackathon deadline that I am probably going to regret. Writing the log is partly accountability and partly self-defence: if I publish what I decided on Tuesday, I’m less likely to quietly rewrite history on Friday when it doesn’t work.

So the entries will be honest about the parts that go badly, which I expect to be most of them. If that sounds like something you’d want to read, the sign-up below is the whole distribution strategy. There isn’t a second one.
